Goals of this model:

  1. Compensate all faculty, instructors, and research supervisors for their work.
  2. Create a robust, non-Departmental pathway for students to complete the Shared Gen Ed’s WBL requirement.
  3. Give a ‘course’ framework for WBL, even when it’s 0-credit, to support assigning and assessing students’ requirements, tracking completion on degree audits, etc.
  4. Provide students with a 0-credit, no-cost option for completing the WBL requirement, especially over the summer.
  5. Give students the option to earn academic credit for summer work experiences, provided the experience is approved by their department and the student then completes all additional requirements of their department’s internship course.

The model includes:

SEED: no incremental cost

Faculty-led research: $500/student from the Activity Fee

Existing for-credit internship courses: $6,250 for a full section, prorated at $250/student

Existing 0-credit internship courses: $2,500 for a full section, prorated at $100/student

Credit-bearing fall supplements for summer experiences: $150/student

An option for students to enroll in their department’s credit-bearing internship course in the Fall semester after completing a 0-credit summer internship. During the first weeks of this course, students complete the academic and integrative assignments required to receive major credit for their experience.

CE-300: Work-Based Learning Seminar: $2,500 for a full section, prorated at $100/student

A 0-credit course, administered by the Career Center and instructed by interested faculty, career center staff, and other qualified professionals. This course would, in theory, alleviate Departments’ and faculty’s concerns about ensuring every student completes a Shared Core requirement with an internship course in the student’s home department.

CE-300: Work-Based Learning Seminar

CE-300 provides a flexible, scalable course structure for students completing approved internships or other work-based learning. It is designed to create opportunities for instructor engagement with students’ work-based learning without requiring departments to assume the administrative burden of supporting every student's experience. It ensures consistent oversight for students while preserving departmental internship courses and other established WBL pathways.

Structure and Enrollment

Students will submit an experience for approval by designated staff in the Career Center prior to the start of their intended WBL term. If approved, the student will be registered for CE-300 for the following/relevant term.

One large Workday section of CE-300 will house all registered students until a specified point prior to the start of the term, when the enrollees are divided into sections of 20-25 based on major and work placement. These sections may then be assigned to field-relevant instructors when possible.

Instruction, Support, and Compensation

Suffolk faculty are welcomed and encouraged to teach CE-300, alongside qualified Career Center staff, other University staff, and potentially alumni or external adjunct instructors, subject to University approval. Assignments are supplemental and based on enrollment and instructional needs.

The Career Center coordinates approval of students’ experiences, enrollment, common course materials, performance evaluation tools, employer support, and escalation of workplace concerns. The Center also serves as the primary point of contact for students with urgent concerns requiring timely attention.

CE-300 instructors provide academic guidance, student support, and assessment rather than managing placements. Responsibilities include leading an initial synchronous online orientation (offered at two or more times to accommodate students’ schedules), meeting individually with each student (typically via Zoom) near the midpoint of their experience, responding to questions or concerns within two business days, and assessing final requirements, including student and supervisor performance evaluations, an updated résumé, and a reflection and updated Career Plan v3.0. Students are directed to the Career Center for concerns requiring more immediate attention.

Sections will have a target enrollment of 20-25 students. Instructors will be compensated at $100 per student ($2,000-$2,500 per section), with a floor of 15 students/$1500.

All assignments are supplemental and based on enrollment and instructional needs.
